Reactor physics
What Is Reactor Physics?
You cannot look inside a reactor core. We compute it.
Jooil Yoon · KAIST Presidential Interview
This is an APR1400 core — the reactor Korea exported to the UAE. Control rods absorb neutrons; withdraw them and the chain reaction begins. The core heats — but not evenly. Where does the heat come from? A neutron is born fast and slows down by colliding with hydrogen in the water. Uranium-235 absorbs the slow neutron and splits; the fragments shake the fuel around them — that is the heat. Reactor physics turns this microscopic theory into a calculation of neutron behavior across the whole core. You cannot look inside a reactor core. So we compute it.

Yoon & Joo, J. Nucl. Sci. Technol. 45, 668 (2008) · Joo, Yoon & Baek, Ann. Nucl. Energy 36, 85 (2009)
Jooil Yoon · KAIST Presidential Interview
This is how we compute the core you just saw. We cannot solve the whole core in full detail, so we do it in two steps. First, the lattice code solves 2D neutron transport on one fuel assembly and passes homogenized few-group cross sections to the core code. Second, the 3D core analysis code runs a 3D nodal calculation of the whole core on coarse nodes, then takes each node — one assembly at one height — and reconstructs its 2D pin-by-pin power; combined over all nodes this gives the 3D pin power of the whole core. I developed the core functionality of that code — the nodal method and the pin power reconstruction, both published — and built the core design code system that links the two steps, and took it through licensing in Korea and the UAE. Before that, Korea licensed this software from Westinghouse.

Lim, Kwon & Yoon, Nucl. Eng. Technol. 57, 103596 (2025) · Yoon, Lee, Joo & Kim, Nucl. Eng. Technol. 53, 3543 (2021)
Jooil Yoon · KAIST Presidential Interview
We are past the catch-up stage. The conventional nodal method computes one average per assembly and reconstructs the rods. MONTEX and SPHINCS compute every fuel rod directly. MONTEX is a GPU Monte Carlo code: a lattice calculation takes one second instead of thirty minutes. It produces pin-wise cross sections with SPH equivalence factors, and SPHINCS solves the whole core pin by pin — four seconds for an i-SMR core, one minute for a full cycle. As far as I know, no one else is developing a pin-wise code system at production level, ready for design and licensing.

Against the whole-core transport reference, on six benchmark cases, pin power error drops by a factor of two and a half, the peak pin error by almost three, and the eigenvalue error by five and a half — from over a hundred pcm to about twenty. Technical Issues in i-SMR and PWR Cores
Two open issues — and my technologies that solve them, each on a concrete path to application.
Issue 1 · Flexible operation i-SMR · PWR
Issue 2 · i-SMR core design soluble-boron-free · long-cycle operation
Jooil Yoon · KAIST Presidential Interview
Two technical issues define the next decade of light water cores. First, flexible operation: to work with renewables, PWRs and the i-SMR must follow load daily, and the nuclear data no longer fits the core's history. My isotope-indicated spectrum shift method corrects that. Second, the i-SMR core itself: no soluble boron and a long cycle. MONTEX and SPHINCS compute every fuel rod directly, and the HIGA absorber holds reactivity flat without boron. These are not proposals. HIGA is patented and is to be adopted as the official i-SMR burnable absorber; MONTEX and SPHINCS are backed by four published papers and planned for i-SMR design; a patent for the spectrum shift method is in preparation.

Advanced reactors break both the geometry and the neutron spectrum the light water methods were built for. I already have three solvers running: HEXANUS computes pin powers directly on hexagonal cores within fifty pcm of transport in hundredths of a second; VENUS, developed with KAERI for fast reactors, matches the SNR-300 and VVER-440 benchmarks within fifteen pcm; and CYNUS handles the cylindrical molten salt reactor, coupled with OpenFOAM for multiphysics.

This is the system I want to build for the i-SMR. At the top, an in-house language model — built with KAIST's AI groups — takes a design request in plain language.
